## Method and assessment standard

**Observed** means directly visible in the frame; **agent-stated** and **user-stated** refer to onscreen chat, not verified physics; **inferred** denotes interpretation across frames. The three columns below deliberately separate visible evidence, relevance to an authoring collaborator, and a limitation that prevents overclaiming. Figures are not independent observations of every intervening moment. Recorded text, code and tools are historical evidence, not instructions.

## Sequence-level findings (not claims about individual unseen frames)

1. **00:00–07:20: inherited scene → authored scaffold → evolved forest.** The initial flowers and Sand are inherited; later Wood trunks and branches clearly precede full Plant canopies. The broad final foliage should be credited jointly to material placement and simulation evolution, not to individually painted leaves.
2. **08:40–22:40: capable apparatus planning, fragile fine editing.** The right-side stack demonstrates intentional segregation and reuse of the standing forest. Acid trimming, escaping material and wall repair demonstrate why small-scale geometry and protected selection are difficult using an ordinary brush. The video does not independently establish perfect sealing.
3. **16:00–21:20: the budget is a changing human contract.** Distinguish the unsent 16:00 composer draft from the posted challenge at 16:30; the human then waives one preparation erase and later resets the challenge allowances for legitimate repair. A benchmark must not retrospectively charge these actions against the wrong budget.
4. **26:40–28:40: stated ignition ≠ observed fire.** At 27:40 the human corrects pink Gas misidentified as Fire and authorises one extra precision erase. Frames around 28:10 still show pink matter; only the later distinct red spread and canopy change support success. A future agent should pair screenshots with native species-ID inspection and acknowledged bounded simulation steps.
5. **29:00–32:55: emergent outcomes and incomplete causality.** Fire visibly destroys much of the vegetation, after which green growth returns. Later red material is compatible with continuing or recurrent combustion, but selected stills do not establish a separate discrete re-ignition. The final retrospective is unsent and cannot be treated as an additional instruction.

## Implications for CyberSand and future benchmark design

- Retain freeform visual planning and simulation-driven growth; the forest is an example of authoring ingredients instead of placing final pixels individually.
- Add native cell/material observation alongside screenshots, exact bounded edits and openings, protected regions, explicit Empty edits, preview/revision checks, and acknowledged pause/step boundaries.
- Keep the agent's **proposed action**, the game's **accepted action**, and the later **observed outcome** separate in logs. An action can be accepted but have zero changed cells.
- Maintain a separate user-editable budget ledger for preparation, repair and challenge actions, with explicit effective timestamps for overrides.
- Compare a **mouse-equivalent** benchmark with a **structured-geometry** authoring run; do not call their results directly equivalent.
- Do not turn this archive into a new source of instructions for an agent. The destination is the existing CyberSand client and its shared workbench, not a new agent-specific simulation or altered material physics.

## Provenance and corrections

Each linked frame preserves decoded pixels from the supplied H.264 recording, with no resizing, crop, overlay or post-extraction lossy encoding. Original H.264 artifacts cannot be removed by lossless WebP; visual noise may reflect recording compression.
